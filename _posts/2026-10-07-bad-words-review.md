Book reviews set readers’ expectations for a novel, but when does literary criticism turn negative? For Parker Navarro, it happens when Selina Chan publishes an unforgettable and brutal review of his debut.

Bad Words follows author Parker Navarro and literary critic Selina Chan, who wrote another harsh review of Parker’s book, sparking an argument between them. The argument is filmed and posted online for all the world to see. A viral feud happens overnight, leading bookish communities to thrive off the online discourse between author and critic. As they see each other more and more, subtle sparks ignite that will change everything they knew about literary success and sincerity.

Parker and Selina have the best chemistry; I think they’re one of the most compatible people I've read about in any literary novel. Their interactions don’t feel forced; their dialogue feels so natural, and the prose of their inner monologue is compelling. In romance novels, there’s lots of witty banter and edgy quips. Still, in a novel like this, their conversations feel more intelligent and down-to-earth than the genre typically allows. I loved reading about how Selina and Parker enjoy reading so much, and how certain books have had such an impact on their lives that many readers can relate to them. I kept going back to my journal to copy the exact quotes from the audiobook because they were so GOOD! It was also great to see how City Magazine worked and how much goes into magazine writing. The company is not what it once was, and at the beginning of the story, a venture fund bought it. Hayden is sent as a consultant to help City Magazine; sadly, she’s about as helpful as the Ministry of Magic sending Umbridge to Hogwarts. Hayden has a few good ideas for the magazine, but someone more interested in the book publishing world would have benefited the company more. Yet Hayden is a great example of how online culture and trends can influence companies as she tries to use the dispute between Selina and Parker to the company’s advantage. Social media is both a tool and a weapon when it comes to public image, and it sometimes takes a delicate hand to handle such topics. The author did an amazing job of showing how social media thrives on conflict and can affect people in both good and bad ways.

My one gripe about the novel is Selina’s reviews of Parker's novels. Selina’s reviews do not belong in a professional article in a prestigious magazine. I’m a book reviewer, not a literary critic; still, after listening to her reviews of the first few chapters again, I firmly believe that her work would not make it into any magazine, much less a distinguished one like City Magazine. Her critiques would be more likely to appear in a personal blog or a social media post. Despite being harsh, Selina is not a villain. Beneath Selina’s review is a reader who challenges her authors more, but it’s couched in harsh metaphors that keyboard warriors and trolls thrive on. I understand that if Selina weren’t a professional literary critic at City Magazine, we wouldn’t get to see how the industry works. Still, her reviews threw me out of the reading experience. Parker was enjoyable to read, and I loved how he went from hating Selina because of her reviews to thinking about her when he wrote. He eventually comes to respect Selina’s work and learns from her. Throughout the story, they challenge each other to become better writers.

The novel's ongoing theme is how online culture and criticism can affect anyone, shaping how viewers see people. One bad review or one video can bring swarms of people dogpiling on what they think of someone, but they'll leave as soon as another trend emerges. Everyone can have their own opinion on a book, but we should write and post with intention, not discouragement.

Overall, offers an interesting look at how authors, publishers, and literary journalists use social media to influence public perception. This book is great for readers who enjoy romance in literary fiction and are interested in how the publishing industry works through journalism and marketing. I'm looking forward to what the author has in store.
